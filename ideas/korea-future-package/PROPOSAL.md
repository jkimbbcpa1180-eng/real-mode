# Korea Future Package: Master Policy Proposal (CC0)

One plan that ties together a secured future for children, help for families now, a clean-energy funding stack and
an environmental fix, with the goal of raising Korea's birth rate. Every number marked **(example)** is illustrative only.
Nothing here has been costed. Not legal or financial advice. Figures were checked on 2026-10-08 (KST); sources are inline.

## 1. Goal and problem
- **Goal:** more Koreans feel safe enough to have the children they want, paid for by clean-energy gains, not new debt,
  while the air and climate get better for those children.
- Korea's total fertility rate was **0.80 in 2025** (preliminary), up from 0.75 in 2024, with 254,500 births
  ([Statistics Korea briefing](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=156745912)). Still far below 2.1.
- **Housing:** a state think tank (KRIHS) found that a 1% rise in median home prices means about 0.002 fewer births
  per woman ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/3033697)).
- **Education race:** families spent **KRW 27.5 trillion** on private education (mostly 학원, private academies) for
  school-age kids in 2025, an average of KRW 458,000 a month per student
  ([Statistics Korea, 2026-03-12](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=156748552)).
- **Mothers' careers:** Korean mothers' earnings fall by more than 41% by five years after a first birth, while fathers'
  barely change ([Hwang & Yoo, ACAES](https://acaes.us/asia-economics-blog/child-penalty-korea)).

## 2. How the pieces fit
```
[Clean grid + public solar/wind + carbon fees + won stablecoin interest + chip royalty + recycling]
        |  only measured, audited money goes in
        v
[Future Fund]  --(locked 10-year citizen bond, see grid dividend doc)
        |      --(Education Fund: tuition promised at birth)
        v
[Child born] -> free tuition (any field, extra for shortage fields) -> 2-year service job -> free to choose after
[Parents now] -> early-years rent relief + free after-school programs + 48-week wage match
```

## 3. A secured future for every child
1. **Tuition guaranteed by law at birth.** Every child born after the start date gets free university tuition in any field.
   The promise is written into a charter law (section 7), so parents can count on it when deciding to have a child.
2. **Extra stipend for shortage fields** such as semiconductors, engineering and medicine, e.g. KRW 300,000 a month
   **(example)**. Students choose freely; the stipend nudges, it does not force anyone into STEM.
3. **Two-year service contract with a guaranteed job.** After graduating, the student serves 2 years in a real job at a
   fair wage, e.g. at least the median graduate starting salary **(example)**. The job is guaranteed, not just the duty.
4. **Graduates choose the employer** from a public list of approved companies, labs, hospitals and public agencies.
5. **Switch careers after year 1.** Year 2 can be served in another approved field or a public role. The state pays for
   a short retraining course if needed. Switching must be the graduate's own choice: an employer that pushes someone out
   and calls it a "career change" pays a fee, like the firing fee in the wage match proposal.
6. **Fair exit.** Leaving early means repaying only the unserved share of tuition (1 of 2 years served = half), interest-free.
7. **Parental leave never counts against you.** Leave either pauses the clock or counts as time served (graduate picks).
8. **Regional bonus.** Serving outside the Seoul capital area earns a bonus, e.g. +10% pay **(example)**,
   to spread jobs and ease Seoul housing pressure.

**Precedents already in Korea**
- **계약학과 (contract departments):** Samsung Electronics runs hiring-guaranteed semiconductor departments with 7 universities
  (incl. SKKU, Yonsei, POSTECH, KAIST) and SK hynix with 3 (Korea Univ., Sogang, Hanyang). Students get full scholarships and
  a job after graduating ([Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20250905_0003317252)). This plan scales the idea up and
  lets graduates choose and switch.
- **국가장학금 (national scholarship), 2026:** pays more the lower the family income. In families with 3+ children, the third
  and later child gets full tuition up to income band 8; the first and second get KRW 4.65–6.1M a year in bands 1–8
  ([KOSAF](https://www.kosaf.go.kr/ko/scholar.do?pg=scholarship05_12_10&ttab1=1),
  [Ministry of Education](https://www.moe.go.kr/boardCnts/viewRenew.do?boardID=294&boardSeq=105272&lev=0&m=020402&opType=N&page=1&s=moe&searchType=null&statusYN=W)).
  The National Assembly Budget Office has suggested extending multi-child support to 2-child families; that is not law yet
  ([Mudeung Ilbo/Newsis](https://www.mdilbo.com/newsroom/6634003)).

## 4. Help families now, not only in 18 years
A promise that pays out 18 years later does little for a couple deciding today, so the package pairs it with help in the early years:
- **Early-years rent relief:** e.g. a rent or jeonse-interest subsidy for families with a child under 6 **(example)**.
- **Cheaper 학원 through free after-school programs:** expand free, good-quality after-school classes so families need fewer
  academies. Only 36.7% of students used 늘봄학교/after-school programs in 2025 (same Statistics Korea source as section 1).
- **Fair wages:** the [48-week wage match](../wage-match/PROPOSAL.md) raises low pay with a per-child extra and a firing fee.

## 5. Funding stack: no single source
| Source | How it feeds the Future Fund | Honest note |
|---|---|---|
| Grid / superconductor savings | Measured savings split between the locked 10-year citizen bond and the Education Fund, e.g. 50/50 **(example)** | Small today (see below) |
| Public solar, offshore wind, rooftops | A share of power sales from state-owned plants | Needs years of building |
| K-ETS carbon permits | A share of auction revenue | Already goes to the Climate Response Fund |
| Public won stablecoin | Interest on the reserves behind the coin, plus tiny AI-to-AI payment fees | Depends on adoption and rates |
| Chips and AI | A royalty on state-supported chip profits or AI data-center revenue | Cyclical industry |
| Recycling | Sales of recovered materials and biogas | Modest but steady |

**Grid dividend rules** (details in the [grid dividend bond proposal](../grid-dividend/PROPOSAL.md)):
- **Pay out only measured savings,** verified by independent meters and an auditor. The fund never spends money it doesn't have.
- **Bridge funding:** existing scholarship budgets pay at first, and the grid dividend takes over step by step as savings are proven.
- **Invest like a pension fund:** diversified, with a fixed payout rule, so returns keep paying for future children.
- **Scale check:** Korea used about 600–630 TWh of electricity in 2025 (KHNP: 595.5 TWh generated; Ember: 628 TWh)
  ([KHNP](https://khnp.co.kr/eng/contents.do?key=4603), [Ember via World Power Monitor](https://worldpowermonitor.com/countries/south-korea/)),
  and grid losses were about 3.3% in 2024 ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.ELC.LOSS.ZS)). Halving those
  losses would save about 10 TWh; at KRW 150/kWh **(example)** that is about KRW 1.5 trillion a year **(example)**, small next
  to the KRW 27.5 trillion families spend on private education. That is why the fund needs every source below.

**Carbon permits (K-ETS):** auction revenue was KRW 187.6 billion in 2024, below the KRW 289.6 billion forecast
([eToday](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2507501)); the 2026 budget expects KRW 765.1 billion
([Newspim](https://m.newspim.com/news/view/20250924001221)). Today it funds the Climate Response Fund (기후대응기금), so a share
for families would need a legal change.

**Public won stablecoin**
- **Status:** as of 2026-10-08 Korea has **no** won-stablecoin law. Lawmakers' bills await committee review, and the Financial
  Services Commission says the government's Digital Asset Basic Act is in final inter-agency talks; who may issue is the main dispute
  ([Daily Hankook](https://daily.hankooki.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=1413333), [Newspim](https://newspim.com/news/view/20260923000764)).
- **Where the money comes from:** issuers earn interest on the reserves. Circle (USDC) had USD 2.64 billion of reserve income in
  2025, 96% of its revenue ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000187604226000032/final_circlereportsfourt.htm)).
  Tether reported over USD 10 billion of 2025 profit, with about USD 141 billion in US Treasury exposure plus other investments
  ([Tether](https://tether.io/news/tether-delivers-10b-profits-in-2025-6-3b-in-excess-reserves-and-record-141-billion-exposure-in-u-s-treasury-holdings/)).
  A public issuer would send that interest to the fund. E.g. KRW 10 trillion in circulation x 2.5% = KRW 250 billion a year **(example)**.
- **Reserves:** mostly cash and short-term government bonds, plus a small gold slice as a crisis hedge, e.g. 5–10% **(example)**.
  Gold alone swings in price, so a coin backed only by gold would not hold a steady won value. Gold-backed tokens exist
  (each PAX Gold token = 1 troy ounce of vaulted gold, [Paxos](https://www.paxos.com/pax-gold)), and the Bank of Korea holds
  104.4 tonnes of gold ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10849098)).
- **Not backed by chips or computing power.** GPUs lose value fast and chip prices boom and crash. A coin backed by them
  could break like TerraUSD, a Korean-founded coin whose 2022 collapse wiped out about USD 40 billion
  ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-73), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/terrausd-creator-do-kwon-be-sentenced-over-40-billion-crypto-collapse-2025-12-11/)).
  Chips help through the **royalty** instead, plus **discounted AI computing credits** for schools and students as a perk.
- **AI-to-AI payments:** AI agents paying each other for data and computing could settle in the coin, with a tiny fee to the fund.

**Recycling**
- **Battery, solar-panel and fab-material recovery** (lithium, cobalt, silicon, gases, metals) lowers the cost of new renewables.
- **Metals:** recycled aluminium uses about 95% less energy than new aluminium ([International Aluminium Institute](https://international-aluminium.org/landing/aluminium-recycling-saves-95-of-the-energy-needed-for-primary-aluminium-production/)).
- **Food waste to biogas:** Korea recycled 96.8% of its food waste in 2023 using weight-based fees and smart bins, but only
  about 16% became biogas (42% animal feed, 33% compost) ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/dec/18/smart-bins-measuring-food-waste-south-korea)).
  More biogas plants would turn more of it into power and heat, with gas sales going to the fund.
- **Deposit refunds paid in the won stablecoin** for bottles, cans and old electronics, which also grows use of the coin.

## 6. Energy plan to power the country
Bioenergy (including waste) supplied only about 3% of Korea's power in 2025 (Ember, link above), so recycling is support,
not the main source. Denmark (about 91%) and Uruguay (about 98%) got most of their 2025 electricity from renewables, against
about 10% in Korea ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/share-electricity-renewables)); Korea has heavier
industry and less land.
- **Offshore wind and solar** as the biggest new share: west and south coast wind, solar on rooftops, reservoirs and farmland.
- **Nuclear for steady power:** about 29–31% of generation in 2025, depending on the source
  ([Ember via World Power Monitor](https://worldpowermonitor.com/countries/south-korea/), [KHNP](https://khnp.co.kr/eng/contents.do?key=4603)).
- **Storage:** recycled EV batteries; **pumped-storage hydro** in the mountains, which pumps water uphill when power is spare and
  runs it back through turbines at peaks, returning roughly 70–80% of the energy
  ([IEA Energy Storage factsheet](https://iea-es.org/wp-content/uploads/public/FactSheet_Mechanical_Pumped_Hydroelectric.pdf)).
  Korea already runs plants such as Cheongpyeong (400 MW) and Yangyang (1,000 MW) ([KHNP](https://www.khnp.co.kr/eng/contents.do?key=548)),
  and is building more, e.g. Yeongdong (500 MW, target 2030) ([Yonhap](https://www.yna.co.kr/view/AKR20250402090200064)).
  Seasonal mountain ice (section 10) can top up reservoirs in spring. Old mines and existing reservoirs are the best sites.
- **Superconducting lines** move power from the coasts to Seoul and the fabs. A 1 km, 23 kV HTS cable has run commercially in
  Yongin since 2019, but long lines are still pilots (see the grid dividend doc).
- **AI smart grid:** AI forecasts demand and weather and balances supply in real time; humans stay in control.

## 7. Security protocols
No system is unbreakable; the aim is that abuse is very hard and caught fast. No single point of failure, everything visible.
- **Reserves:** independent custodian separate from the issuer; real-time proof of reserves; public audits every quarter.
- **Multi-party signatures:** moving reserves needs keys from several independent bodies, e.g. the Bank of Korea, the National
  Assembly and an outside auditor (3 of 3 or 3 of 4 **(example)**). No single person can move funds.
- **Withdrawal caps in law,** and any large movement waits through a public delay window, e.g. 7 days **(example)**.
- **Smart contracts:** at least two independent code audits before launch, plus a public bug bounty.
- **Emergency pause** that needs several key holders to agree, so one insider can't freeze or drain the system.
- **AI flags, never moves money.** It watches for fraud and odd patterns; humans approve every action.
- **Privacy-preserving ID:** payments tied to verified residents without leaking personal data; stops duplicate claims.
- **Locks stay locked:** bond and tuition money can't be cashed out early, except the rules in the grid dividend doc.
- **Clawback for fraud,** with penalties.
- **Charter law needing a supermajority** to change the fund's rules, so no single government can raid it.
- **Public real-time dashboard** of every won in and out.

## 8. Heavy industry: make it green, don't ship the pollution abroad
Moving steel, petrochemicals and shipbuilding abroad would clean Korea's numbers but not the planet: the pollution moves,
often to places with weaker rules ("carbon leakage"), and Korea loses jobs and the tax base that funds this plan.
- **Hydrogen steel:** POSCO's HyREX replaces coal with hydrogen. A 300,000 t/year demonstration plant in Pohang is planned
  for 2028, with commercialization targeted by 2030 ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14479),
  [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/04/the-hydrogen-stream-posco-bhp-advance-hydrogen-reduction-ironmaking-technology/)).
- **Electric process heat:** electric furnaces and industrial heat pumps running on clean power.
- **Carbon capture** where emissions can't be avoided.
- **Clean exports:** the EU's carbon border tax (CBAM) has applied to steel, aluminium, cement and more since 1 January 2026;
  certificates for 2026 imports go on sale in February 2027 ([European Commission](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en)).
  Clean Korean steel becomes an advantage.
- **Offshore only with clean power:** if an energy-heavy step moves abroad, only to places with abundant clean power
  (e.g. Australian solar) and with clean materials shipped back.

## 9. Phase 1 quick fixes (a bandaid that buys time)
Cheap, fast steps that help now and build what the long-term plan needs.
- **Expand the seasonal coal limits.** Since 2019, each Dec 1–Mar 31 Korea halts some public coal units: in 2025–26 up to 17
  in winter and 29 in March, with up to 46 more running under output caps ([MCEE briefing](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=156731226),
  [Hankyoreh](https://www.hani.co.kr/arti/society/environment/1246461.html)). Extend and enforce it more strictly.
- **Shore power and cleaner fuel at ports.** Busan has installed shore-power hookups since 2019, but Korean ports don't require
  ships to use them and use has been low ([Electric Times, 2022](https://www.electimes.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=302216)).
  Require plug-in at Incheon and Busan, help ships retrofit, and require cleaner fuel near the coast.
- **Diesel trucks and buses:** retrofit filters or switch to electric. A fast cut to city dust.
- **City forests:** trees and green walls along highways and industrial zones.
- **National real-time sensor network** with public alerts, scaling up local pollution logs so fixes target the worst spots.
- **Joint China smog warnings.** Korea and China already share forecasts at least daily (MCEE briefing above); upgrade to shared
  real-time monitoring and joint early warnings. One model study put China's share of Korean PM2.5 at about 45% a year
  and about 60% in Jan–Feb ([Kumar et al. 2021](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/17314/1/1-s2.0-S1352231021003642-main.pdf)).

## 10. Climate and sea level: what is and isn't possible
- **We can't lower sea level with today's technology, only slow the rise.** That comes down to cutting emissions (sections 6, 8).
- **Restore tidal flats (갯벌):** they store carbon and buffer storm surges. Getbol, Korean Tidal Flats has been a UNESCO World
  Heritage site since 2021 ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1591/)).
- **Glacier protection research:** white fleece covers on Switzerland's Rhone Glacier cut melt by about half, but only over a
  small patch, and can't cover a whole glacier ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/2016/11/stefan-schlumpf-hidden-landscapes-rhone-glacier/)).
- **Mountain ice storage ("cryoforming") is a water tool, not a sea-level fix.** Ice stupas in Ladakh freeze winter water into
  cones that melt in spring for farms; one of the largest held about 7.5 million litres, about 7,500 tonnes
  ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/27/ice-stupas-of-the-ladakh-desert-an-ingenious-solution-to-water-scarcity-a-picture-essay)).
  Greenland lost about 264 billion tonnes a year on average in 2002–2025 ([NASA](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/31156/)), which would take
  about 35 million such stupas. In Korea it could ease spring droughts, freezing with spare night-time solar and wind power.
- **Rejected: evaporating excess water with heat.** Boiling off water takes about 2,257 kJ/kg
  ([nuclear-power.com steam tables](https://www.nuclear-power.com/nuclear-engineering/thermodynamics/steam-tables/)), about 627 kWh per tonne,
  while lifting a tonne 100 m takes only about 0.27 kWh (1,000 kg x 9.81 m/s² x 100 m). Evaporation costs about 2,300 times
  more than a dam could return, and more still if the water starts cold.
  Better: store flood water high up, release it through turbines, recharge groundwater.
  **The useful version:** desalination using waste heat from fabs, steel mills or nuclear plants, which is thrown away anyway.

## 11. Limits and caveats
- **Benefits alone raise birth rates only modestly.** Reviews find small positive effects at best, often shifting when births
  happen more than how many ([Gauthier 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/poprpr/v26y2007i3p323-346.html)). Childcare for under-3s
  and ongoing support after birth work better than one-off grants ([Luci-Greulich & Thévenon 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/eurpop/v29y2013i4d10.1007_s10680-013-9295-4.html)).
  Housing, long work hours and the career penalty for mothers matter most, which is why sections 3–4 come as a package.
- **Superconducting grids are mostly pilots.** Room-temperature superconductors don't exist yet.
- **Grid-loss savings are small:** Korea's losses are already about 3.3%, so the grid dividend alone can't pay for tuition.
- **Stablecoin income depends on adoption and interest rates,** and there is no won-stablecoin law yet.
- **K-ETS revenue is already earmarked** and has come in below forecast.
- **Uncosted:** no estimate yet of tuition for every child, service-job supply, or rent relief. An independent body such as the
  National Assembly Budget Office should cost it.
- **Service contracts need guardrails:** enough real jobs in every field, no forced placements, protections for disabled students.
- **Timing:** tuition promised to a baby born in 2027 is paid around 2046; the fund must survive many governments.
