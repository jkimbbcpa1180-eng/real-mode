# 48-Week Wage Match: Policy Proposal (CC0)

A one-page proposal for Korea: raise low wages and support families without crushing small businesses.
Every number marked **(example)** is illustrative only. Nothing here has been costed. Not legal advice.

## 1. Problem
- Low pay and the cost of raising children are big reasons young Koreans delay or skip having kids.
- A sharp minimum-wage jump puts the whole cost on employers. After the 2018 (+16.4%) and 2019 (+10.9%)
  increases ([Minimum Wage Commission](http://www.minimumwage.go.kr/english/introduce/minWage.do)), the government
  ran a separate subsidy alongside them to ease the cost for small businesses (see section 9).
- Pure tax credits such as the EITC help workers but give employers no push to raise pay themselves.

## 2. Proposal
A temporary, government-funded **wage match** that works as a bridge to a real raise:
1. The employer pays the normal paycheck. The government adds a set percentage on top for low-wage workers,
   e.g. **10–20% (example)**, paid directly to the worker.
2. The match **phases out as wages rise**, e.g. full match up to 110% of the minimum wage, tapering to zero at
   150% **(example)**.
3. **Extra match per child**, e.g. +5 percentage points per child under 18, capped **(example)**.
4. The match lasts **48 weeks**. Then the employer either gives a raise to a defined target or pays a fee.
5. During those 48 weeks the job is **bonded**: firing without a justified cause triggers a bigger fee.

## 3. How it works
```
Week 0      Hire or enroll an eligible position. Payroll verified. Match starts (paid to worker monthly).
Weeks 1-47  Employer pays wage; government pays the match (+ child extra). Bond active.
            Unjustified firing  -> bond fee.   Voluntary quit -> no fee, match stops.
Week 48     Match ends. Employer chooses:
              (a) Raise to target  -> no fee, worker keeps take-home pay
              (b) No raise         -> non-raise fee, paid into the matching fund
After 48    Position cannot restart the match (see section 6). Raise stays on payroll records.
```
**Raise target (proposed rule):** the employer's own wage at week 48 is at least what the worker was taking home
with the base match (wage + base match), so pay does not drop when the government steps back.
The per-child extra is family support and could continue separately rather than count toward the target.

## 4. Fees
- **Non-raise fee:** must cost more than the raise it replaces, or owners will simply pay it.
  E.g. 1.5x the annual cost of the missing raise **(example)**.
- All fees flow back into the matching fund.
- Fees can be paid in installments, with interest-free terms for hardship-exempt businesses (section 7).

## 5. The 48-week bond
- Firing the worker within the 48 weeks triggers a **bond fee larger than the non-raise fee**,
  e.g. repay all match money received plus a penalty **(example)**, unless the dismissal is ruled justified.
- **Venue:** Korea's Labor Relations Commission (노동위원회), which already rules on unfair dismissal claims
  (Labor Standards Act art. 28, claims within 3 months; [NLRC](https://www.nlrc.go.kr/nlrc/minwon/CmmnEventRequest/EltrDlivPrivacy.do),
  [law.go.kr](https://law.go.kr/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1000610057)). It is faster and cheaper than court.
  Court remains the fallback on appeal.
- **Justified causes defined up front:** theft, serious misconduct, documented repeated no-shows.
  A short checklist of required evidence keeps honest owners safe.
- **Voluntary quits carry no fee** for anyone. Proven forced resignations count as firings.
- Business closure or bankruptcy: fee reduced or waived after review, so failing shops are not pushed under.

## 6. Anti-gaming safeguards
- **Match tied to the position or total payroll, not to each new hire.** Firing at week 47 and hiring someone new
  does not restart the clock. The position's remaining weeks carry over to the replacement.
- Alternative: cap matched headcount at the business's baseline eligible payroll, so only real jobs count.
- **Payroll verification:** cross-check every matched worker against employment-insurance enrollment and tax-office
  wage filings. Flag ghost workers, family-member padding and sudden headcount spikes.
- Paying the match **directly to the worker** stops employers from pocketing it.
- Minimum-hours rule so one job can't be split into several part-time slots to multiply the match.
- No reducing the base wage or hours during the 48 weeks to inflate the match. Random audits and clawback for fraud.

## 7. Small business protection
- **Hardship exemption or reduced fee** for very small businesses, e.g. under 5 workers **(example)**,
  with low profit proven through tax filings.
- Options: lower fee, installments, or an extended deadline to reach the raise target.
- Simple one-page application through existing employment-insurance channels, no lawyer needed.

## 8. Funding
- General tax revenue, plus recycled non-raise and bond fees.
- Possible offsets: savings in other wage or employment subsidies if this replaces part of them.
- **No cost estimate here.** Proper costing is needed before anything moves forward: number of eligible workers,
  take-up, fee recovery and child extras. An independent body such as the National Assembly Budget Office could do it.

## 9. Related existing programs
- **Korea 근로장려금 (EITC):** an annual cash payment through the National Tax Service for low-income households
  with wage, business or religious income. For 2025 income: under KRW 22M (single), 32M (one earner), 44M (dual earner),
  with an asset limit. A separate child credit (자녀장려금) exists ([NTS](https://www.nts.go.kr/nts/cm/cntnts/cntntsView.do?cntntsId=7783&mi=2450)).
- **Korea 일자리안정자금 (Job Stability Fund):** started January 2018 to offset the minimum-wage jump. Firms under 30 workers
  received up to KRW 130,000 per month per worker paid under KRW 1.9M/month
  ([Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/policy/2018/01/04/20180104004009)). About 2.64M workers were
  supported in 2018 ([Business Post](https://www.businesspost.co.kr/BP?command=article_view&num=110209)). By 2021 it was
  down to KRW 50,000 (70,000 for under-5 firms) ([korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148865988)).
  This proposal differs: it pays the worker, has an end date and requires a raise.
- **US EITC:** a refundable federal tax credit for low- and moderate-income workers, larger with children.
  Tax year 2025 maximum: $649 with no children up to $8,046 with three or more
  ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/earned-income-tax-credit/earned-income-and-earned-income-tax-credit-eitc-tables)).
- **Minimum wage:** KRW 7,530/hour in 2018 (+16.4%) and 8,350 in 2019 (+10.9%) ([Minimum Wage Commission](http://www.minimumwage.go.kr/english/introduce/minWage.do)).

## 10. Open questions and risks
- **Coverage gap:** LRC unfair-dismissal claims currently apply only to workplaces with 5+ regular workers.
  The bond would need a rule or a simplified LRC track for smaller shops.
- **Deadweight:** some raises would have happened anyway, so public money pays for them.
- **Wage holding:** owners might keep pay just under the phase-out line. The raise target and fee must outweigh this.
- **Overlap with 근로장려금:** how the two interact without creating benefit cliffs or double counting.
- **Who qualifies:** part-time, platform, seasonal and migrant workers. Child extra needs family-record checks with consent.
- **Recession:** whether to pause the week-48 fee during a downturn.
- **Admin load and fraud:** payroll audits cost money. Fee collection from closing businesses may be weak.
- **Birth-rate effect is uncertain.** Wages are one factor; housing, work hours and childcare matter too.
