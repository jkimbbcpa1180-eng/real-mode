# Grid Dividend Bond: Policy Proposal (CC0)

A one-page proposal: when the grid gets more efficient, every resident gets an equal share of the gain,
saved in a locked bond account. Every number marked **(example)** is illustrative only. Nothing here has been costed.
Not legal or financial advice.

## 1. Problem
- Grids lose power as heat between the power plant and the home. Losses were about **6.5% of output worldwide
  and 3.3% in Korea** in 2024 (US: 5.3%) ([World Bank EG.ELC.LOSS.ZS](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/EG.ELC.LOSS.ZS)).
- Upgrades that cut those losses or bring cheap clean power to cities create real value, but it usually stays with
  utilities or disappears into general budgets. Residents see no direct share.
- Young people struggle to build savings for a home deposit or to start a family, and cash payments are often spent at once.

## 2. Honest status of superconductors
- **Room-temperature superconductors do not exist yet.** LK-99 (2023) was not one: replication work traced its
  "superconducting" signs to a copper sulfide impurity ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-02585-7),
  [Matter](https://www.cell.com/matter/fulltext/S2590-2385(23)00564-7)).
- **High-temperature superconducting (HTS) cables already work**, cooled with liquid nitrogen (about -196°C).
  KEPCO and LS Cable began commercial operation of a 23 kV, 50 MVA HTS cable over about 1 km between the
  Shingal and Heungdeok substations in Yongin in 2019 ([Yonhap](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20191105005500320),
  [IOP paper](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6668/ab6ec3), [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/overhead-transmission/article/21171587/koreas-kepco-commercializes-superconducting-transmission-solution)).
- So this design **starts now** with today's gains (HVDC lines, grid storage, HTS cables, loss-cutting upgrades)
  and **scales up automatically** if room-temperature superconductors ever arrive.

## 3. Idea
1. Measure the energy **saved** (lower losses) and **newly delivered** (clean power the upgrade made possible).
2. Put a set share of that value into a public **Grid Dividend Fund**, e.g. 50% (example).
3. Every resident gets an **equal dividend** that grows with how much the system supplies.
4. Instead of cash, each share goes into the person's **locked bond account**. It unlocks after **10 years**,
   or earlier if the account has grown **more than 30%**.

## 4. How the money flows
```
[Grid upgrade: HVDC / storage / HTS cable / future superconductor]
        |  energy saved + new clean energy delivered
        v
[Independent meters + auditor] --> verified MWh x market price = value created
        |  set share (e.g. 50%, example)
        v
[Grid Dividend Fund] <-- recycled early-withdrawal penalties
        |  invested in a diversified portfolio (grid revenue is only one part)
        |  pays out a fixed % of fund value per year (spending rule)
        v
[Equal share per resident] --> [Personal locked bond account]
        |                                  |
        |    unlock: 10 years, or >30% growth, or approved exception
        v                                  v
  children's shares locked until adulthood     cash out / home deposit / keep invested
```

## 5. The 10-year locked bond and the 30% unlock
- Each year's dividend buys units in the person's bond account. **Each yearly deposit has its own 10-year clock** (proposed),
  so money unlocks in a rolling way instead of all at once.
- **30% early unlock:** a deposit can be withdrawn early once it has grown more than 30% **after inflation** (proposed),
  so inflation alone can't trigger it and nobody cashes out at a loss.
- After unlocking, the owner can withdraw or keep it in the account and keep earning.
- **Tax rule (proposed):** principal is tax-free. Growth is taxed once, at withdrawal, at a simple flat rate
  (rate to be set by law). Money kept invested after unlocking stays untaxed until withdrawn.
  Gains up to the CPI guarantee could be tax-free to protect small savers **(option)**.

## 6. Improvements
1. **Inflation-protected minimum return:** principal is guaranteed and the account earns at least CPI,
   backed by the fund's reserve and, as a last resort, the state. Singapore's CPF already guarantees floors:
   2.5% on the Ordinary Account and 4% on Special, MediSave and Retirement Accounts through 2027
   ([CPF](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/infohub/news/news-releases/government-extends-4-per-cent-interest-rate-floor-on-special-medisave-and-retirement-account-monies-until-31-december-2027)).
2. **Hardship withdrawals:** for serious medical costs, job loss or disaster, with a small penalty,
   e.g. 3% (example), paid back into the fund.
3. **Penalty-free family withdrawals:** for a first-home deposit or the birth or adoption of a child,
   tying the bond to the birth-rate goal.
4. **Sustainable payout rule:** the fund pays out a fixed share of its value each year, e.g. 3% (example),
   averaged over several years, so the principal survives for future generations. Norway spends about the fund's
   expected real return, now 3% ([regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/en/topics/the-economy/economic-policy/economic-policy/id418083/)).
5. **Independent, transparent board:** fixed staggered terms, professional managers, full holdings published,
   independent annual audit. Lawmakers cannot raid the fund or change the payout rule without a supermajority (proposed).
6. **Measurement rule:** "saved" energy = measured loss rate before the upgrade (multi-year baseline) minus the
   loss rate after, times energy carried. "Supplied" energy = clean MWh delivered that the upgrade made possible.
   Metered by independent revenue-grade meters, verified by an auditor, and priced at the wholesale market price.
7. **Equal shares:** one equal share per legal resident, with a minimum residency period, e.g. 1 year (example).
   **Children's shares are locked until adulthood (19 in Korea)**, except the penalty-free family uses above.
8. **Diversification:** the fund invests in a broad mix of bonds and global assets, not only grid revenue,
   so savers are not hurt if one project underperforms.
9. **Phased start:** a pilot city or region first, e.g. one HTS or HVDC corridor (example), with a public
   report after 3 years (example) before going national.

## 7. Precedents
- **Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend:** paid to residents every year since 1982 from oil wealth. The 2026 dividend is
  $1,000 plus a $200 energy relief payment ([Alaska DOR](https://dor.alaska.gov/docs/default-source/press-releases/2026-004-dor-announces-2026-pfd-amount.pdf?sfvrsn=3d93cd6a_1)).
  The amount is set by the legislature each year and has been far below the statutory formula, a warning about
  political control ([Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/economy/2025-09-19/the-2025-alaska-permanent-fund-dividend-will-be-1-000),
  [Alaska Beacon](https://alaskabeacon.com/2026/09/30/permanent-fund-dividends-will-be-distributed-to-alaskans-starting-this-week/)).
- **Norway's Government Pension Fund Global:** oil money saved and invested worldwide. The fiscal rule spends
  about the expected real return, 3% (cut from 4% in 2017) ([regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/en/topics/the-economy/economic-policy/economic-policy/id418083/)).
- **Singapore CPF:** locked personal accounts with guaranteed minimum interest and approved uses such as housing
  ([CPF](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/infohub/educational-resources/how-do-your-cpf-savings-work-for-you)).
- **Korea 청년도약계좌 (Youth Leap Account):** a 5-year savings account for ages 19–34, up to KRW 700,000 a month,
  with a government contribution and tax-free interest. New sign-ups closed in December 2025, and the
  청년미래적금 replaced it in 2026 ([Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20251125_0003416169),
  [korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148973066)).

## 8. Risks and open questions
- **Small dividends at first:** today's efficiency gains are modest; Korea's losses are already low (about 3.3%).
  The early dividend may be small until bigger upgrades or superconductors arrive. No cost or payout estimate here.
- **Who owns the savings:** KEPCO, ratepayers or taxpayers could all claim the value. The split needs a law.
- **Guarantee cost:** a CPI floor can cost the state money in bad markets. It needs a reserve and stress tests.
- **Liquidity crunch:** many unlocks in one year (e.g. after a market boom triggers the 30% rule) could force asset sales.
- **Measurement gaming:** inflated baselines or double counting with other subsidies. Independent audit is essential.
- **Residency rules:** migrants, people who move abroad, and what happens to a locked account when someone dies.
- **Overlap** with existing savings programs and welfare benefits: avoid cliffs and double counting.
- **Political risk:** Alaska shows dividends can be cut by budget fights. The no-raid rule must be strong.
- **Superconductor timing:** may take decades or never come. The design must be worth it without them.
